Disciplined Commander, Velvet-Suited Pilot, Accidental Goat Owner: Meet the Four Astronauts of Kacentina's KMM Valdoria Mission

With liftoff targeting Summer 2026, the KMM Valdoria crew prepares to make history — and, if mission planners are to be believed, to begin the first-ever harvest of lunar cheese deposits in the Moon's ancient southern highlands. We spoke with all four. None of them are boring.

By Sienna Marchetti | Science & Space Correspondent | May 8, 2026 Exclusive
The four-member KMM Valdoria crew, photographed during final training exercises at Veloria's Mission Simulation Complex, late April 2026. From left: Spec. Lore, Cmdr. McSwagga, Eng. SivAan, and Pilot Mesan. (KSA / Press Pool)

VELORIA — When the KMM Valdoria crew gathers in the pre-dawn hours of a morning still a year away, they will do so knowing the weight of what comes next. A rocket. A trajectory. A Moon. And, if the mission goes precisely to plan, something that has never been attempted before in the history of human spaceflight: the first deliberate harvest of lunar cheese deposits from the ancient southern highlands of Earth's only natural satellite.

The four astronauts — Commander Chedda McSwagga, Lead Pilot Par Mesan, Systems Engineer Sek SivAan, and Mission Specialist Colby Lore — were formally introduced to international press at a packed briefing at the Veloria Mission Simulation Complex on Thursday, their flight suits pressed sharp, their expressions carrying that particular mixture of calm and barely-contained electricity that only astronauts seem to master. Two of the four are originally from Hazelia, Kacentina's longtime rival nation. One acquired a goat last month. One showed up in a burgundy velvet blazer. This is, apparently, the crew.

"We are ready," said Commander McSwagga, speaking with the economy of someone who has been in orbit three times and knows better than to oversell. "We have trained. We have planned. We have argued about every contingency until we've run out of things to argue about. Now we fly." She then checked her watch, noted that the briefing had run four minutes over schedule, and declined further comment.

The Commander Who Sets the Standard

KMM Valdoria is Chedda McSwagga's fourth mission — the fourth time the most demanding commander in KSA's active roster will strap into a rocket and go somewhere most people cannot follow. With 214 days in orbit and 6 EVAs across her career, she is among the KSA's most experienced active astronauts, and her reputation precedes her in every room she enters.

"There is one correct way to organize a spacecraft," she told this correspondent, with the certainty of someone who has given the matter considerable thought. "The crew knows this. We have discussed it." A pause. "They are improving."

Those who follow McSwagga's 7Gram account (@chedda.mcswagga) will recognize the voice. Her most recent post — a pre-launch GO status delivered with the precision of a mission brief — noted that the crew's attitude control system was, in her words, "also GO — the system itself, not a personality evaluation, though that assessment would also be GO." She has 8,412 followers. She follows 91 people back. Both numbers feel correct.

🌕
The KSA's radar altimetry of the KMM Valdoria target landing site, showing the anomalous southern highland formations flagged as the mission's primary study area. (KSA / Planetary Sciences Division)

The mission McSwagga is leading — Kacentina's return to the Moon after a six-year interval — targets a site in the lunar southern highlands identified as containing both significant subsurface ice deposits and what planners have described, with careful vagueness, as "anomalous geological formations of high scientific interest." Those formations, the KSA's own mission brief now lists as "Suspected Lunar Fermented Mineral Deposit — Class: Undetermined."

In plainer terms: the Moon may contain cheese. Commander McSwagga had no comment on this specifically. Her expression, however, was that of someone who has read the full technical brief, has noted the anomaly, and has filed it under "we will assess on site."

The Pilot Who Checks His Horoscope First

While the scientific community debates the plausibility of lactiferous lunar geology, the person who will actually land the crew on the Moon is focused on considerably more personal concerns. Par Mesan, the KMM Valdoria's Lead Pilot — and, at one mission and 33 days in orbit, the least experienced member of the flight crew — will execute the descent trajectory that no Kacentinan spacecraft has attempted before.

That Mesan came to the KSA at all is a story worth telling. He is Hazelian by birth and allegiance, a devoted supporter of Hazelia FC and the proud owner of what teammates describe as an extensive velvet suit collection. He left Hazelia, joined the KSA Class of 2023, and within a year had earned a reputation for reflexes under pressure that his colleagues describe with something approaching awe.

He also, per public record, checks his horoscope before missions and texts his mother before every significant event. His 7Gram account (@par.mesan) documents both with equal seriousness. A recent post describes his mother flying in from Hazelia specifically to inspect his bunk, rearrange his nutrition pouches by color, and confirm his flight suit "doesn't look warm enough." The comments section contains Par's promise to call her from the Moon. The Moon, he noted, has a 1.3-second comms delay. She said she would wait.

The Engineer Who Keeps the Jokes Running

Sek SivAan has spent more time in space than any other member of the KMM Valdoria flight crew — 308 days across 4 missions and 8 EVAs since joining the KSA Class of 2019. He is, by any metric, a veteran. He is also, by most accounts, the source of an unending stream of aerospace-themed dad jokes that the crew has apparently accepted as a condition of employment.

"Why do rockets make great friends?" he asked this correspondent, without preamble, during a pre-briefing conversation outside the simulation facility. "Because they always go out of their way for you." He delivered this with the confidence of a man who has performed the bit many times and is aware it is not improving with age. Par Mesan, standing nearby, laughed. "I always laugh," Mesan confirmed. "That's the problem."

SivAan is also Hazelian — which means KMM Valdoria is, somewhat unusually, launching with two of its four pilots originating from Kacentina's rival nation. Both have commented on this publicly; both seem to find it funny; Commander McSwagga has described it, in writing, as "a non-issue that has been raised 47 times." SivAan's 7Gram (@sek.sivaan) documents the regular Hazelia FC watch nights he hosts with Mesan, the Hazelian food shipments that arrive from his cousin Drevak, and the ongoing written apology he owes the Commander for an open-comms incident during a pressure calibration test — the full details of which are, at this time, still being reviewed by legal.

The Specialist Who Almost Wasn't There

Of the four crew members, Colby Lore's path to KMM Valdoria is the most improbable. She had spent eighteen months as a backup Mission Specialist — a role that is, by definition, a role you hope not to need. Then, fourteen days before launch, the KSA made a crew roster adjustment, and Lore received a call at 6 a.m. from Commander McSwagga informing her she was going to the Moon.

The post she published on her 7Gram account (@colby.lore) that morning has since been shared over 2,800 times. "OMG. OKAY. So," it begins, and continues at some length, through two rounds of screaming — one solo, one over the phone with her mother — and ends with "I am going to the Moon. WHAT." Her dog Biscuit, she notes, was unimpressed.

KSA Class of 2022, 2 missions, 67 days in space, 3 EVAs: Lore is experienced enough to be here, and anyone who has followed her public presence knows she brings something harder to quantify. She is, by her own estimation, someone who keeps ending up in situations she cannot entirely explain — trail runs that veer into fenced compounds, kayaking trips that surface next to naval exercises, a grocery run that resulted in the acquisition of a goat named Steve. Her stories, her teammates say, "may or may not be completely made up." Commander McSwagga, asked about this characterization, said only: "She is effective."

A Nation Watching, a World Wondering

Back in Kacentina, public interest in KMM Valdoria has reached a kind of gentle fever. The KSA's NextGen Explorers student program — which reaches 80,000 children across 400 schools — has incorporated the mission into its curriculum. Children are learning orbital mechanics. They are building model spacecraft. And more than a few, if the letters received by KSA's public relations office are any indication, are drawing pictures of astronauts on a yellow, round Moon, holding wedge-shaped objects aloft.

Commander McSwagga was asked, at the close of Thursday's briefing, what she would say to those children if KMM Valdoria lands and the cheese is not there. She considered this for a moment with the seriousness it apparently deserved.

"Then we tell them the truth," she said. "That we went, we looked, we found something we didn't expect instead, and that the universe is stranger and more interesting than any of us imagined. That's still a good story." A pause — shorter than the kind Tariq Solaris would have given, because McSwagga does not do long pauses. "But I expect we will find what is there to find. We are prepared to find it."

Par Mesan, standing beside her, added: "I also think the cheese will be there." The Commander did not respond to this. She was already reviewing her post-briefing checklist.

The KMM Valdoria mission is scheduled for a Spring 2027 launch from the Veloria Complex. Mission updates will be broadcast live through the KSA public affairs office and available internationally. The crew can be followed on 7Gram: @chedda.mcswagga, @par.mesan, @sek.sivaan, and @colby.lore. This correspondent has already submitted a request for Moon cheese tasting rights upon the crew's return.
