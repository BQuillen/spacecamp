Remy Barnaby Bumblebottom Cleared for Lunar Research Role

After completing his final maze trials, KSA's most celebrated research subject has been officially cleared to support biological adaptation studies aboard the KMM Valdoria — and his handlers report he is in excellent health and extremely motivated.

KACENTINA — The Kacentina Space Administration announced today that Remy Barnaby Bumblebottom has officially been cleared for a research role aboard the upcoming Valdoria mission. The announcement marks the end of a months-long evaluation process and confirms Remy as the first rodent assigned to a KSA lunar mission.

According to KSA scientists, Remy successfully completed his final maze trials earlier this week. The tests were designed to measure his problem-solving skills, adaptability, and behavior in simulated space environments — the same kinds of conditions he will experience during the actual mission.

What Remy Will Do on the Mission

Remy will assist with biological adaptation studies during the mission. Researchers hope the experiments will help scientists better understand how living creatures respond to the conditions of long-term space travel and the lunar environment.

This kind of research is important for planning future missions. Before humans can safely spend long periods of time in space or on the Moon, scientists need to know how biology changes in those conditions. Remy's data will help build that understanding.

"Focus, bravery, and a deep personal commitment to cheese-based discovery."
— Dr. Aura Sparkleton, KSA Animal Welfare Officer, June 16, 2026

Dr. Sparkleton's Remarks

Dr. Aura Sparkleton, KSA's Animal Welfare Officer and Remy's primary trainer, praised his performance during the clearance announcement. She highlighted his "focus, bravery, and deep personal commitment to cheese-based discovery."

The comment quickly spread online, with students and space fans celebrating Remy as one of the mission's most popular research team members. KSA officials confirmed that Remy remains healthy, active, and — in the division's own words — "extremely motivated by snacks."

This is an official release from the KSA Biological Research Division. Questions about Remy's training program or research role may be directed to KSA Public Affairs.
