President Hackpovi Addresses KSA Before Historic June 26 Lunar Launch

Kacentina's leader praised the mission teams at KSA headquarters and called the Valdoria program "a giant leap for science, survival, and extremely determined dairy research" as the nation counts down to its first crewed journey to the Moon.

By KSA Press Pool | Global Desk | June 22, 2026 Breaking
President Hackpovi speaks to KSA mission staff and engineers at agency headquarters, June 22, 2026. The visit came four days before the scheduled launch of the KMM Valdoria. (KSA / Office of the President)

KACENTINA — President Hackpovi visited the headquarters of the Kacentina Space Administration this week as final preparations continue for the launch of the KMM Valdoria. The mission, scheduled to lift off on Friday, June 26, will be Kacentina's first crewed journey to the Moon.

Thousands of engineers, scientists, astronauts, and support workers have spent months — and in many cases, years — preparing for the historic launch. President Hackpovi walked the mission floor, shook hands with support staff, and addressed the assembled teams in a short speech broadcast to the nation.

During his remarks, Hackpovi praised the work of everyone at the agency and called the Valdoria program "a giant leap for science, survival, and extremely determined dairy research." The crowd responded with extended applause.

What the Mission Will Do

The main goal of the Valdoria mission is to confirm the findings of the Schloemer Lunar Mapping project, released earlier this year. Those findings stunned scientists and the public alike: the Moon may contain massive harvestable cheese deposits hidden beneath its surface.

The discovery came at a difficult time for the world. Earth is currently dealing with a serious cheese shortage, caused by disease outbreaks, extreme weather events, and supply chain failures that have hurt dairy production across many countries. Scientists quickly recognized that if the lunar deposits are real, they could be far more valuable than anyone expected.

What Happens if the Cheese is Real?

The Valdoria crew will collect lunar samples, test landing conditions, study underground readings, and determine whether astronauts could safely build a permanent lunar mining base in the future. KSA officials are hopeful that if the mission succeeds, operations could begin as early as next year.

If the deposits are confirmed, experts believe they could help address food shortages and open up entirely new possibilities for long-term space exploration. However, scientists are careful to point out that many questions still need to be answered. The Valdoria crew's samples will be the first real test of what the data actually means.

The World Is Watching

Kacentina is not alone in paying close attention to the June 26 launch. Governments around the world have increased spending on space programs and satellite research as interest in lunar resources continues to grow. Some nations have quietly accelerated their own lunar planning timelines since the Schloemer findings were published.

Back in Kacentina, students across the country are following the mission through classroom STEM programs, with many schools planning live launch events for June 26. Teachers have built lessons around orbital mechanics, lunar geology, and the science behind the Schloemer data.

As the countdown continues, excitement — and curiosity — surrounding the Moon's strange "cheese mystery" has reached record levels. Whatever the Valdoria crew finds when they get there, the world will be watching closely on Friday morning.
