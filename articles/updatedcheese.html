Schloemer Lunar Mapping Team Releases Updated Cheese Deposit Model

New analysis of satellite and deep-surface sensor data has identified several promising underground regions near the planned Valdoria landing zone — though scientists say direct sampling is still needed before anything can be confirmed.

By KNBS Science Desk | Kacentina National Broadcasting Service | June 18, 2026 Science

KACENTINA — Scientists working with the Kacentina Space Administration have released an updated model showing possible underground cheese deposits near the planned Valdoria landing zone on the Moon.

The new data comes from the Schloemer Lunar Mapping Team, a group of researchers who have been studying unusual readings collected from orbiting satellites and deep-surface sensors positioned near the Moon. The team's earlier findings first raised the possibility of lunar cheese deposits and drew attention from scientists around the world.

Confirmation MissionKMM Valdoria (June 26, 2026)

What the New Data Shows

According to researchers, several areas beneath the lunar surface showed patterns that may point to large hidden deposits. The updated model gives scientists a clearer picture of where those areas are located relative to the Valdoria crew's planned landing zone.

Scientists say more testing is needed before the discovery can be confirmed. Officials also warned that the readings could still have another explanation, and that astronauts will need to examine the area directly during the upcoming mission before any conclusions can be drawn.

"Extremely difficult to explain unless the Moon is, somehow, very snackable."
— Schloemer Lunar Mapping Team Researcher, June 18, 2026

A Quote That Caught Everyone's Attention

Despite the careful scientific language in most of the report, one researcher admitted the results are "extremely difficult to explain unless the Moon is, somehow, very snackable." The comment stood out from the rest of the technical release.

The statement quickly became popular online, spreading across social media and news feeds as people reacted with a mix of amusement and genuine curiosity. Excitement around the upcoming Valdoria mission — and the unusual discoveries connected to it — continues to grow as the June 26 launch draws closer.
