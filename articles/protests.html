Animal Welfare Group Holds Downtown Protest

Members of the Federation for Assisting Rats and Turtles — known as F.A.R.T. — marched through the city this week to protest what the group called "unfair treatment of small animals in research programs." The demonstration remained peaceful throughout the afternoon.

By CNN Public Affairs Desk | City News Network | June 23, 2026 Local
F.A.R.T. members march through downtown Veloria on Tuesday afternoon. The group carried signs calling for stronger legal protections for animals used in research programs. (City News Network / Press Pool)

VELORIA — Members of the Federation for Assisting Rats and Turtles, also known as F.A.R.T., gathered downtown this week to protest what the group called "unfair treatment of small animals in research programs."

The organization marched through the city carrying signs that read "Protect Every Creature" and "Science Needs Compassion." Speakers at the event called for stronger laws protecting animals used in laboratory testing and commercial experiments.

A Peaceful Afternoon March

Organizers said the protest was meant to raise awareness about the treatment of rats, turtles, and other small animals often overlooked in public discussions about animal welfare. The demonstration remained peaceful, with families, students, and local volunteers joining throughout the afternoon. Several protesters handed out flyers explaining the group's mission and encouraging people to support animal rescue efforts.

City officials reported temporary street closures while the march moved through nearby intersections. No arrests or injuries were reported.

"Science Needs Compassion."
— Protest sign, F.A.R.T. demonstration, June 23, 2026

A Familiar Face in the Crowd

Toward the end of the protest, attendees noticed a woman believed to be Dr. Aura Sparkleton — the KSA's Animal Welfare Officer and handler for KMM Valdoria research rat Remy Barnaby Bumblebottom — speaking quietly with several event organizers near the crowd. She did not make a public statement before leaving the area.

The KSA did not respond to a request for comment on Dr. Sparkleton's presence at the event. The KMM Valdoria mission, which includes Remy Barnaby Bumblebottom as a biological research payload, is currently scheduled to launch Friday, June 26.
