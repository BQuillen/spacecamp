2012 — LEO · Duration: 14 Days · Crew: 2
Jojo Vanguard
Kacentina's First Step into Space

The Jojo Vanguard mission officially launched Kacentina into the space age. For the first time in national history, a KSA rocket successfully carried astronauts into orbit and safely returned them home. The mission tested life-support systems, communication technology, and orbital navigation while proving Kacentina could compete with the world’s top space programs. More importantly, it inspired an entire generation of scientists, engineers, and future astronauts to look toward the stars.

2015 — LEO / Climate Orbit · Duration: 28 Days · Crew: 3
Clouthouse I
Environmental Monitoring Mission

As climate concerns grew across the world, the KSA launched the Clouthouse Monitoring Mission to study Earth’s atmosphere, coastlines, and environmental changes from orbit. Specialized satellites tracked CO₂ levels, storm development, ocean temperatures, and coastal erosion to help Kacentina better prepare for natural disasters and food shortages. The mission proved that space technology could improve life back on Earth—and gave KSA scientists valuable experience operating long-term observation systems in orbit.

2017 — LEO Station · Duration: 270 Days · Crew: 4
Station Founding — KSS Kacentina
Establishment of the Kacentina Space Station

The construction of the KSS Kacentina marked the nation’s greatest engineering achievement to date. Built piece by piece in orbit, the station became a permanent research platform where astronauts could live and work in space for months at a time. Crews aboard the station monitored Earth systems, tested advanced technology, grew experimental crops in microgravity, and prepared for future deep-space missions. The station would later become the command hub for Kacentina’s lunar ambitions.

2020 — GEO / MEO · Duration: 90 Days · Crew: Uncrewed
C7 Network Deployment
Satellite Communications Constellation

The C7 Network Deployment mission launched a powerful satellite network designed to provide global communications, weather monitoring, and planetary security systems for Kacentina and its allies. The satellites created reliable internet access for remote regions while also helping monitor severe storms, environmental hazards, and cyber threats around the world. KSA officials described the project as “a shield and signal for the future,” proving that space infrastructure could strengthen both national security and everyday life.

2024 — Lunar Mapping · Duration: 30 Days · Crew: Uncrewed
Schloemer Lunar Mapping
Lunar Survey — Orbital Mapping

Originally launched to search for frozen water reserves near the Moon’s southern pole, the Schloemer Lunar Mapping mission made a discovery nobody expected—and almost nobody believed at first. Deep-scanning sensors detected massive underground deposits of organic material with chemical properties nearly identical to aged cheese. At the time, Earth was facing a catastrophic global cheese shortage after crop failures and bacterial contamination devastated dairy production worldwide. What began as a routine lunar survey quickly became the most important resource discovery in human history. After months of testing and verification, KSA scientists confirmed the impossible: large portions of the Moon appeared to contain harvestable cheese deposits.

2026 — Moon Mission · Duration: 3 months · Crew: 4
KMM Valdoria
Moon Mission - Cheese Harvest

Launching this month, the KMM Valdoria mission will send a four-person crew to the Moon to confirm the findings of the Schloemer mission and identify the safest location for future lunar operations. The astronauts will conduct surface scans, collect core samples, test mining equipment, and begin planning for long-term habitation. The mission also includes biological experiments involving Remy Barnaby Bumblebottom, KSA’s famous research rat. If successful, Valdoria will prove that lunar cheese extraction is possible and pave the way for a permanent human presence on the Moon.

2027 — Lunar Surface · Duration: 365 Days · Crew: 9
Des Kakpovi Lunar Base
Humanity's First Lunar Base - Cheese Mining Operation

The Des Kakpovi Lunar Base mission represents the next stage of Kacentina’s lunar program: establishing a permanent Moon base and beginning full-scale cheese mining operations. Named after President Hackpovi’s grandfather, a factory worker who famously said “the future belongs to whoever shows up first,” the base will include living quarters, research labs, mining equipment, and launch systems for transporting lunar resources back to Earth. KSA leaders believe the mission could help stabilize the world’s cheese supply, create new scientific opportunities, and secure Kacentina’s place as the leading space power on Earth.
